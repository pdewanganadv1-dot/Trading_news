Why the signal matters

Same rule, different years and regimes

GO bars, the indicator's strike, all entry windows. The edge is lumpy: it lives in the years and volatility regimes where a late breakout still finds cheap premiums.

Reading it live

The timetable says when and which offset; the exact strike comes from the indicator on the day. In TradingView, ExpiryEdge_BuyScore prints the suggested strike and its estimated premium in the status table and in the alert JSON (strike, est_premium). In Python, scripts/live_indicator.py --index NIFTY --source dhan --watch prints the same in the strike column. scripts/otm_timetable.py regenerates every number on this page.

What these numbers are. Real index paths, modelled premiums: a Black-Scholes kernel on the expiry-day variance profile with reactive IV, the CAS variant reserving 25 % of the day's variance for the auction, a premium floor (₹1.5 NIFTY, ₹3 BANKNIFTY, ₹5 SENSEX) and costs of ₹0.5 / ₹1 per side. Returns are net; "exit" means the 15:10 bar or 60 minutes, whichever comes first. The August-2026 real-data check through Dhan is what turns these into exact numbers — until then treat the ranking (late GO bars, nearest cheap OTM, never the 3rd strike) as the robust part and the means as model-dependent. SENSEX is shown for completeness only: 45 Friday-regime expiry days, 37 GO bars, negative — the buy-score does not transfer to it.
